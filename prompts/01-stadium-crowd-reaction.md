# Stadium Crowd Reaction

A restrained crowd-reaction shot built around a gradual expression change,
broadcast cinematography, and subtle character motion.

## Overview

- **Model:** Wan 3.0
- **Category:** Sports / Cinematic
- **Workflow:** Video prompt
- **Style:** Photorealistic baseball broadcast

## Prompt

```text
Photorealistic MLB baseball stadium broadcast footage. A young Southeast Asian woman with warm medium skin, long straight dark hair falling loose past her shoulders, gold hoop earrings, and a delicate pendant necklace sits in the stadium stands. She wears a black spaghetti-strap top with a dark oversized cardigan slipping off one shoulder. Blue stadium seats fill the background.

Broadcast telephoto framing, shallow depth of field, indoor stadium floodlights, and an ESPN-style scoreboard HUD along the bottom of the frame.

Motion sequence: She sits relaxed in her seat, her gaze drifting toward the field with a slightly parted mouth and curious eyes as she processes something happening in the game. Her expression gradually softens into a quiet, knowing half-smile. Her hair catches slight air movement. Background spectators make subtle, natural movements in soft bokeh.

Camera: A static-to-slow-push telephoto shot with subtle broadcast compression and minimal handheld drift. The camera emphasizes natural skin texture, dark reflections in her eyes, and understated charisma.

Style: Cinematic detail, cool indoor stadium lighting with warm fill on the face, natural motion blur on the hair, photorealistic skin texture, and authentic crowd-camera energy.
```

## Why This Prompt Works

- The expression develops in a clear sequence rather than changing abruptly.
- The slow telephoto push keeps attention on the subject without excessive camera motion.
- Small movements in the hair and background prevent the frame from feeling frozen.
- Lighting and depth-of-field instructions establish a recognizable broadcast look.

## Tips

- Replace branded broadcast graphics with a generic sports overlay if exact text or logos are not important.
- If you use a source image, preserve the subject's identity, clothing, and seat position throughout the shot.
- Keep the expression change subtle; exaggerated emotion will alter the intended candid tone.
