# Motorcycle Action Scene

A character-driven action workflow combining a reference-based source image with
a fast, low-angle motorcycle tracking shot.

## Overview

- **Model:** Wan 3.0
- **Category:** Action / Character
- **Workflow:** Image prompt + video prompt
- **Style:** Photorealistic action cinema

## Image Prompt

```text
Cinematic, ultra-realistic action photograph of a joyful woman riding a yellow scrambler-style motorcycle at high speed on a dusty rural road, with the full motorcycle visible. Use the provided face reference image to preserve her recognizable facial features and likeness. She wears a white motorcycle helmet, a red Nike jacket, gloves, and blue jeans. Behind her, a chimpanzee clings securely to her waist, wearing aviator-style tinted goggles with its mouth wide open in excitement and joy.
```

## Video Prompt

```text
Cinematic, ultra-realistic action video. A joyful woman rides a yellow scrambler-style motorcycle at high speed on a dusty rural road, with the full motorcycle visible. The camera tracks from a low side angle and then moves slightly behind her.

The woman wears a white motorcycle helmet, a red Nike jacket, gloves, and blue jeans. Preserve her recognizable facial identity from the supplied reference image. A chimpanzee clings securely to her waist from behind, wearing aviator-style tinted goggles with its mouth wide open in excitement and screaming joyfully.

Dust and dirt burst beneath the tires in slow motion, with particles flying through the air. Golden-hour lighting, cinematic motion blur, realistic physics, wind moving the jacket fabric, and subtle camera shake. Shallow depth of field, dramatic contrast, film-grade color, high-detail realism, and an action-movie style.

Maintain correct rider posture, hand placement, anatomy, motorcycle geometry, and passenger contact throughout the shot.
```

## Why This Prompt Works

- The source-image step establishes identity, wardrobe, vehicle, and passenger placement.
- The low side-tracking camera makes speed legible while keeping the full motorcycle visible.
- Dust, fabric, and camera motion provide secondary cues of acceleration.
- Continuity constraints target common anatomy and vehicle-geometry failures.

## Tips

- Use only facial references you have permission to use.
- Keep the helmet design open enough for the intended facial features to remain visible.
- Replace branded clothing with an original design for brand-neutral work.
- Reduce particle density if dust begins to obscure the motorcycle or rider.
